The Story Behind the Works

Some works are created to be seen. Others are created to survive.

I never went to prison intending to create art.

But during one of the most difficult periods of my life, I created two works that would eventually become witnesses to a chapter I could never have imagined living through.

One was interrupted, and will never be finished.

The other began as a cry of despair and became proof, to myself, that my mind could stay free even when my body was not.

Neither was created for an exhibition. Neither was created to be sold.

Today, both remain as reminders of a time when my freedom was restricted, but my hope of returning to my family remained.

Work 01

The painting that will never be finished.

The Unfinished Freedom

During my imprisonment, I attended painting classes. For a brief period, I was able to create something colourful in a place where so much of my life felt uncertain.

I began painting a landscape. I never finished it. That chapter ended before the work did, I was moved to another prison, and I will never go back to the place where I started it.

I have since decided that I will never paint again. This painting will therefore never be finished. What you see is all there will ever be, and the fact that it stopped is now part of the work itself.

Work 02

The paper that counted the days.

34 Days to Freedom

When I arrived at another prison, my family did not even know I had been transferred. I had no television and no telephone available to speak to them. I did not know when I would be able to explain where I was.

I took a sheet of paper, the reverse of an official prison document, and began to draw. What I put on it was a cry of despair and a cry for help. To God I asked the same thing again and again: "Why? Why? Why don't You listen to me?"

And on the same page, beside that cry, I wrote the opposite truth: "My body might be in prison, but my mind is free." The despair and the hope sit together on one sheet. It was my way of saying that wherever my body was held, my mind did not have to stay there with it.

Later, I used the same sheet to count the days until I could go home. Every mark recorded another day that had passed. A drawing became a calendar. An ordinary piece of paper became a personal record of waiting for freedom.

Behind the Works

I left prison. I left painting behind.

J. C. Dias is a Brazilian-born author and business leader based in the United Kingdom. During a period of imprisonment, he created two original works that recorded different moments of an intensely personal journey.

His experiences have since informed his writing and reflections on adversity, leadership, faith and rebuilding life.

These works are not the beginning of a conventional painting career. They are surviving objects from a chapter of his life that has ended.
